The product diagram is the selected integration, {{KitchenTimerSystem::kitchenTimer}}. The four diagrams after it open one subsystem each.

The user interface binds its display port to the display and connects the display and the sounder to the power port. The four switches are one button definition used four times. Each switch is mounted on the printed board. One contact is ground. The other contact is a sense net, pulled up to the cell rail by a 100 kΩ resistor and tied to a microcontroller pin. The firmware debounces that pin into a button event.

Control mounts the microcontroller, the 32.768 kHz crystal, two 18 pF load capacitors, a 100 nF decoupling capacitor, the four pull-ups, the base resistor and the NPN transistor on the printed board. The board is 60 mm by 45 mm and 1.6 mm thick, two-layer rigid FR-4. The crystal sits between the microcontroller's oscillator pins, with a load capacitor from each pin to ground. The Embassy runtime and its one Rust task are deployed on the microcontroller. The task's PWM pin drives the transistor base through 47 kΩ. The transistor emitter is ground and its collector sinks the piezo. The piezo's other terminal is the cell rail. The tone is 4 kHz.

Power binds the cell terminal to the supply port and connects the reservoir capacitor across the cell. That capacitor is mounted on the printed board. It supplies the piezo's high-frequency pulses so the alarm-mode current of 3 mA is the current asked of the cell. The capacitor does not source the alarm current for the whole alarm. The alarm current in the budget is still the current asked of the cell.

The enclosure is the product envelope. The display aperture and the four button positions are attributes of that enclosure. The integration checks that the display outline fits the aperture, that the board fits the enclosure footprint, and that the board, the cell and the wall allowance fit the height.
